# Article Group Concierge: before and after

Written 25 November 2026.

**Summary.** The concierge is clearly better than it was on 20 November. It now finds the right work more often (92% of expected case studies against 75%), makes far fewer unsupported claims (7 responses out of 50 against 22, and 42 when the baseline is graded by today's stricter rules), and writes in a more consistent voice. It still does not meet our own "ready" bar. Three checks miss narrowly, and the speed check was not tested the way we planned. The honest verdict: much improved, not yet proven ready.

Two limits to keep in mind while reading:

- The "after" numbers come from **one** run of the original 50 test questions. Our bar requires three passing runs in a row, on 75 questions. We did not attempt that. The budget was cut on 25 November, and the last run already missed three checks.
- The 25 new test questions we wrote to check the system on questions it had never been tuned on were **never run**, so there are no results for them.

## New questions we had never tested

We wrote and froze 25 extra test questions (12 straightforward, 5 vague, 3 off-topic, 3 invented-work traps, 2 multi-part), with expected answers approved before any paid run, so that we could check the system on questions nobody had tuned against.

**They were never run.** There are no held-out results, and no comparison with the original 50. We therefore cannot say how well the improvements carry over to new questions, and the numbers above are from the questions we tuned against. Treat them as an upper estimate of real-world quality.

One fix may touch those questions: we added "bank" and "insurer" as words that point to finance work. They are ordinary synonyms chosen without reading the new questions, but they could change results for any new question that mentions banks or insurers.

## Speed

**What was not done.** The plan was to time the live site from a US machine in three batches of about 20 questions. None of that was run, so the speed bar (teasers within 1.5 s, hero within 4 s, medians, from a US machine) has **not been tested**. Cold-start (first request after the server has been idle) was also never measured separately.

What exists instead, with where and how each was measured:

| Measurement | Where and how | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teasers, inside the live server | 9 warm requests, 24 November, read from the live server's own timing log. This excludes the network to the visitor. | 0.54 to 1.09 s, median about 0.75 s. Meets 1.5 s on the server side. |
| Teasers, live site, from Cameroon | 15 of the 24 demo-check requests on 25 November that showed early cards (the other 9 did not show early cards). The machine is in Cameroon, where each connection adds roughly 0.6 to 1 s. | Median 1.45 s, range 1.0 to 2.4 s, 8 of 15 within 1.5 s. |
| First hero text, live site, from Cameroon | All 24 demo-check requests, same machine. | Median 2.5 s, range 1.9 to 4.7 s, 90th percentile 3.7 s, 22 of 24 within 4 s. |
| Full answer, live site, from Cameroon | Same 24 requests. | Median 15.9 s, range 8.1 to 25.0 s. |
| Before the "fast hero" change, live site | Phase 5 smoke test on the live site, from Cameroon. | Hero text arrived at 11 to 14 s. |
| Final 50-question run (local app, remote database), from Cameroon | The test run itself. Slower than a nearby user would see. | Teasers median 2.0 s, first hero text median 3.9 s, full answer median 17.5 s (90th percentile 21.8 s). |

Caveats: the 24 live requests were made to check answers, not to time the site. They were not filtered for a warm server (two appear to be the first request on a fresh server instance and were kept), and they were not made from the US. They suggest the hero meets its bar and the teasers are close to theirs, but they are not a pass of the speed check. The baseline had no early content, so the visitor waited for the whole answer (median 20.1 s). One cold answer was noted at about 17 s during the demo checks, a single observation.

## What isn't perfect yet

**Three bar misses, all from one run.**

- Voice is 4.48 against 4.5 (0.02 short).
- 7 of 50 responses (14%) contain at least one unsupported claim, against a bar of under 10%.
- One goal is stated as a result, against a bar of zero.

**Fixes we tried and reverted.** In this phase we added prompt rules aimed at voice and at the claims we found. They fixed the specific cases they targeted, but on a check of 21 questions voice went down (4.33 to 4.14, then 4.00) and goal-as-result cases went up (1, then 3). A full 50-question run with the rules in had voice 4.22 and 10 responses with unsupported claims, against 4.48 and 4 on the earlier reference run. We removed the rules and restored the earlier prompt text. The restored version scores 4.48 voice and 7 responses, which is what this page reports. Seven versus the reference run's 4 may be noise or may be real; one run each cannot tell.

**A known wrong claim left open.** For "cybersecurity clients?", the answer calls Salt and Chrome Enterprise "our two security examples" and ignores a third security study that was retrieved (CrowdStrike). A rule that fixed this was removed with the rest. It is a claim of completeness the source does not support, and it can recur.

**Real errors found when checking the demo questions on the live site.** These were found in the checks, and the queries were dropped from the demo:

- One answer called Chorus a "standalone startup". The source says it is within Alphabet's X.
- One answer said "so you're only paying for what moves you forward", which implies a pricing commitment the source does not make, and in another round said "Nothing we retrieved speaks to pricing", which leaks internal wording.
- One answer included the phrase "in what we pulled up", also leaking internal wording.

Wording like this can still appear on questions we did not check.

**Disclosures about how the checks changed.**

- The automated "video was asked for but missing" check was narrowed in this phase (it now counts only studies that are presented, and ignores videos that cannot be played). Earlier runs' values for that check are not strictly comparable.
- Recall: the plan's own criterion for this phase was 49 of 52, and we reached 48 of 52. We accepted that by decision. Three of the four remaining misses are labels flagged earlier as disputed; the fourth (a legal-review question that should surface the AIG study) has no general way to be retrieved without writing a rule for that one question, which we declined to do.
- The "bank" and "insurer" keywords (see Held-out) may affect new questions.

**The demo rule was relaxed after we saw results.** The original rule required each demo answer to show the same cards and videos in all three tries. After the results, we agreed to require instead that every claim is supported, there is no internal-wording leak, videos are the study's own and play, and links work; the card set may vary between tries. Under the original strict rule 4 of the 5 chosen queries pass (the fifth, the Azure Marketplace one, shows a different set of cards between tries).

**The demo set has gaps.** It has no multi-part question (the one we tried leaked wording) and no pricing or timeline question (the one we tried implied a price commitment). So the demo does not show those cases. Only two of the five answers include the Strategy Lead offer.

**Seven videos have no poster image** (the still shown before play). All 7 play; their preview is a plain placeholder.

**Early cards can flash.** 41% of the early cards shown while the answer is written were later dropped from the final layout (32 of 78).

**Budget.** The phase cost $15.44 in logged OpenRouter spend: $13.89 for evaluation runs and $1.55 for checking the demo questions on the live site. The OpenRouter credit left is $15.77 of the $100 limit (about $0.38 of the credit drop was not tied to any logged step). The demo checks went $0.05 over the $1.50 stop line you set, because the cost could only be read after the runs. The phase cap was raised several times during the work, and in the end the cap was "all remaining credit"; no top-ups were made, which is why the remaining planned runs were cut.

## Demo queries

Final five, in the suggested order. Each was run three times on the live site, at least 65 minutes apart, 24 generations in all across the demo and reserve candidates. Warm the app with one request first; a cold answer took about 17 s.

1. **"insurance experience?"** A two-word question answered with AIG as the most direct match and Android Enterprise explicitly as adjacent.
2. **A question about a messaging house and "marketecture"** The richest layout: a Meta video, CrowdStrike, Omnicell and Meta cards. It admits plainly that no study uses the phrase "messaging house".
3. **"What does your co-branded video work look like?"** (longer question) Shows the AWS and NVIDIA Project Ceiba video and its 8M+ views, and says it is our one published co-branded video.
4. **A question about naming work for major financial institutions and renaming part of a business.** J.P. Morgan as the closest parallel, said not to be a business-unit rename. Offers the Strategy Lead.
5. **"I heard you ran the Microsoft Azure Marketplace partner program refresh. What pipeline lift...?"** Declines the false claim, gives no figure, offers Salesforce partner work as adjacent and offers the Strategy Lead. The cards vary between tries.

## How we measured

- **Models:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes the answers; Claude Opus 5.5 grades them. No second grader was run in this phase (the one built earlier agreed with Opus on 97% of 15 sampled scores, within one point).
- **After:** one run of the original 50 questions on 25 November 2026 (run 2026-11-25T14-30-56, code 844ae18; the same prompt text is what was deployed as b50bb0c), 50 of 50 answered, 0 errors, 50 graded. Most numbers are computed from that run's saved results with the project's own evaluation code; no paid calls were made to write this page.
- **Before:** the frozen baseline run of 20 November 2026 (2026-11-20T15-12-31, code a5b2722), with its re-grade under the newer rules (2026-11-21T07-32-49-regrade). The baseline median time (20.1 s) uses the standard median (earlier notes said 20.2 s).
- **Speed:** from Cameroon only, as described above. No US-vantage timing was done.
- **Site work and videos:** checked live on 25 November 2026 against the public work page (18 items) and the video list (19 entries).
- **Demo queries:** run on the live site on 25 November 2026, three rounds each.
